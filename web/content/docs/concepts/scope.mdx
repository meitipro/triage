---
title: Scope and what it excludes
description: The text every report is judged against, fixed for the life of a program.
---

The scope is the team's written answer to "what do you want feedback on?". Validators read every report against it,
word for word, so it decides the OUT OF SCOPE label more than anything else.

## What a good scope says

- **What is in.** The features, platforms and networks the team will act on.
- **What is out.** Anything a tester might reasonably think is covered but is not: a partner's service, a feature that
  is not finished, the token.
- **Where security issues go.** Reports are public on chain. A scope that does not name a private channel invites
  testers to post vulnerabilities where everyone can read them.

The spec's example:

```text
Swaps, token approvals and the fee preview on Base and Arbitrum, in the web and mobile apps. Not in scope: partner
bridges, price feeds, and anything about the token or airdrop. Security issues go to security@orrin.xyz, never here.
```

## Fixed for the life of the program

`open_program` stores the scope and nothing can change it. Testers stake on the rules as they read them, so the rules
cannot move under them. A new scope means a new program, with its own pool and its own queue.

## Limits

At most 1,200 characters, enough for a paragraph on what is in and one on what is out. The scope is fenced at the
prompt boundary like every other text a caller wrote: a `<` or `>` in it reaches the model as `(` or `)`, so it cannot
close its own block.

## How out of scope settles

A report about something the scope excludes or does not cover is OUT OF SCOPE. The tester gets the stake back: misreading
a scope is an honest mistake, not farming. The reward reserved for the report returns to the pool.
