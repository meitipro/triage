Triage is a feedback and bug report program on GenLayer. A team funds a program and writes its scope, testers stake a
little on each report, GenLayer validators read every report against the scope and the reports already accepted, and
only specific, new, in-scope reports are paid, while low-effort farming loses its stake.

<Callout type="warn" title="Warning">
  Reports are public on chain. Send security vulnerabilities to the program's private contact, never as a report.
</Callout>

## One report, start to finish

A tester files *"Swap button stays disabled after approving USDC"* with steps, the expected and the actual result, and
the program's stake. Filing reserves one reward from the pool. Validators read the report against the program's scope
and its last 25 accepted reports, and agree on one of four labels:

| Label | What it means | The stake | The reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACTIONABLE | Specific, reproducible or clearly reasoned, in scope, and not already accepted | back to the tester | to the tester |
| DUPLICATE | The same issue as an accepted report, which it names | back to the tester | back to the pool |
| OUT OF SCOPE | About something the scope excludes or does not cover | back to the tester | back to the pool |
| LOW EFFORT | Vague, generic, no specifics, or a list of unconnected suggestions | to the pool | back to the pool |

An actionable report joins the team's queue. The tester withdraws the stake and the reward whenever they like.

## Why it needs GenLayer

The decision is a reading judgment: is this report specific enough to act on, is it new compared with what was already
accepted, and is it inside the scope the team wrote? The team paying has a reason to say no, testers have a reason to
game any fixed rule, and a single model can be gamed by whoever learns its quirks. On GenLayer several validators apply
the same public rubric, and a report is paid only when they agree.

The stake does the rest. Honest mistakes such as duplicates and out-of-scope reports get the stake back, only low-effort
reports lose it, so a farmer submitting thousands of generic reports loses money on each one, while the pool that pays
real testers grows.

## What Triage is not

- **Not a bug tracker.** It feeds one: the queue exports as JSON or CSV.
- **Not a security bounty.** Critical vulnerabilities go through a private disclosure channel, which the scope names.
- **Not severity scoring.** Every actionable report earns the same reward; the team tips bonuses for important ones.
- **Not private.** Reports are public, so a scope must not invite sensitive details.
